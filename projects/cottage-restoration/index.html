Cottage Restoration

A cottage renovation undertaken with my business partner on our investment rental property in Grass Valley, CA. Our goal was to transform this structure into a fully self contained living space for a single occupant, with a first floor laundry and storage area and a second floor dedicated to the primary living space.

01As found

These are the listing photos from when we bought the property. The main house adjacent to the cottage is a different project that is underway. As you can see, the cottage on the left is in a disheveled state here.

Inside, the living space was very makeshift. The realtor did a good job of making it look presentable but make no mistake it was really in poor shape.

02CAD Mockup

Our first step was to quickly mock up the structure. My business partner called out measurements while I drew up the building in SOLIDWORKS. We wanted to add a front awning and ensure the proportions looked okay. Also we wanted to render the structure to see what color suited it best.

03Siding & paint

The exterior came first. We sanded the old red paint off the siding, down to bare wood in most places. Some pieces were too far gone and we had to replace some. The prep work was extreme to say the least. We were aiming for an oil based paint job that would last years and years. The prep work was extreme to say the least.

04Bathroom framing repair

The entire bathroom area had sagged significantly over time due to the cantilevered shape of the structure. It was paramount that we got the framing level for our new subfloor and subsequent tile.

From the laundry room below, we jacked the floor back up to level, then added new 4x6 beams alongside the old joists to hold it permanently level. We used 4 beams in total and we are confident our repair will stand the test of time.

05Walls & flooring

Inside, the walls were patched and primed, and new plank flooring went down throughout.

06Bathroom

The shower was built from scratch, including a concrete shower base. The waterproofing we had installed passed during a 48 hour leak test, and was ready for tile.

The finished shower: a glass shower door, glass shelving, and a light blue tile floor pairing beautifully with the sage green subway tile.

07Loft & ladder

A sleeping loft sits over the bathroom and closet, with a ladder and railing made from black iron pipe.

08Kitchen

The kitchen was designed with compactness in mind. We wanted everything to neatly fit the back wall and have a nice window to look out of. We installed a row of cabinets my wife made (she is a cabinet maker), a new 120V stove, and a sky blue tile backsplash.

09Finished

Inside, the main room has the kitchen along one wall, a closet and the loft at the back, and exposed beams overhead with a ductless mini split for heating and cooling.

✓The result

There were countless details that went into this project, far more than I could capture here. Attention to detail was essential because we wanted to create more than just an attractive rental. We wanted a well-built, dependable structure that would stand the test of time and require minimal maintenance. The goal was simple: create an investment that could generate passive income without becoming a constant source of work. Done once, done right. The cottage is now rented, and we're happy to say our tenant loves it.
